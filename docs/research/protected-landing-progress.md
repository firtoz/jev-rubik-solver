# Protecting prior work in early-stage decisions

The cost-aware daisy candidate completed three fresh cross stages but disturbed a solved bottom edge in one. Routine selection knew that edge was protected. The later landing question did not, and instructed JEV to lower a blocked top target with F. The model followed that instruction.

The experiment supplies measured protected bottom slots to preparation and lets JEV choose a U reorientation before observing again. Code does not select that action from the state. Fixed routines remain assistance.

| Component experiment | Result | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One question, explicit rules | 18/20 | One unsafe execute, one safe alternative |
| One question, effect descriptions | 12/20 | Missing applicability restrictions caused additional errors |
| Availability then preparation | 20/20 development, 15/16 unused states | Preparation overrode a correct free-space answer |
| Execute-or-prepare, then preparation only if needed | 36/36 retired development cases | Removes redundant reconsideration |
| Frozen revision, fresh legal configurations | 20/20 | Five cases each for execute, clear, lower and reorient |

The last validation uses short random-turn configurations sampled for category coverage. It is not a full-solve reliability evaluation. Earlier unused states are retired after their failure informed the revision.

Three new random-state cross-stage trials completed in 15, 13 and 17 turns, without disturbing solved bottom edges. They used 224 requests and cost $0.006554436. No trial needed reorientation, so evidence for that particular path comes from the component fixtures and execution boundary test, not these three trajectories.

Four paired full development comparisons completed: three solves at 94, 87 and 83 turns, plus a capped attempt at 87 whose selected final routine would exceed 100. The saved candidate solved all four at 98, 96, 95 and 93 turns. Early stages shortened in all four, but overall completion regressed, so this is not a reliability promotion. Cost was $0.020384952. The broad 5/8 full-solve result and original solver records remain separate. The next experiment tests whether full PLL routines reduce the recurring cost of placing corners and edges separately.

Records: `experiments/protected-landing-v1/`. Exact request bodies, native responses, frozen sources, accounting and verification records are retained. The live policy receives no fixture labels, scramble histories or evaluator-selected actions.

General lesson supported so far: carry constraints into every dependent decision that needs them. Once a model has chosen whether to execute, dispatch that answer rather than asking another model call to reach the same conclusion. The evidence is local to these cube decisions; it does not establish a universal prompting rule.
